This number tracing sheet is a train with ten cars, and each car carries one number. Kids trace the big dotted number on the left side of the car, then color that many things on the right: one sun, two hearts, three stars, and so on up to ten raindrops. A smiling engine pulls the train, and a checkered flag at the end says "You did it!" Type your child's name and the title reads "Sam's Number Train."

## The cars and what they carry

| Car | Things to color | Car | Things to color |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 sun | 6 | 6 balloons |
| 2 | 2 hearts | 7 | 7 flowers |
| 3 | 3 stars | 8 | 8 balls |
| 4 | 4 apples | 9 | 9 leaves |
| 5 | 5 fish | 10 | 10 raindrops |

Every number gets a different picture, so children have to count each group fresh instead of guessing from the last car. Six and up sit in rows of three, which makes them easier to count by touching each one.

## Three trains, from first try to fluent

**Trace and count.** The starting version. Trace the number, say it out loud, color the group. Ages 3 to 5.

**Missing numbers.** Four numbers have fallen off the train (3, 5, 8 and 10). Kids count the pictures in each empty car and write the number in the dotted box. The other numbers are still there to trace, so a child can use the cars on either side as a hint. Ages 4 to 6.

**Countdown 10 to 1.** The train runs backward, from 10 to 1, like a rocket launch. It is a fun way to practice counting backward once counting up feels easy. End by shouting "All aboard!" Ages 5 and up.

Online, the sheet opens with the drawing pen, so kids can trace with a finger or a stylus and tap the undo button to try again.

## A five minute routine that sticks

1. Point to the engine and say "This train is carrying numbers today."
2. For each car, your child traces the number with a finger first, then with a crayon.
3. Count the pictures together, touching each one, then color them.
4. Ride along: after car 10, run a finger back along the whole train and count from 1 to 10 without looking at the numbers.

One train a day for a week, switching versions, keeps it fresh.

## What the CDC says to expect around ages 4 and 5

The <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/5-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CDC milestones for 5 years</a> include counting to 10, naming some numbers between 1 and 5 when you point to them, and paying attention for 5 to 10 minutes during activities. That is about the length of one train. The <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CDC milestones for 4 years</a> include holding a crayon or pencil between the fingers and thumb, not in a fist, and the CDC suggests counting simple things, such as fingers, toys or bananas. The pictures on each car are that kind of counting, just on paper.

Every child learns at their own pace. If you have questions about how your child is developing, talk with your pediatrician.
