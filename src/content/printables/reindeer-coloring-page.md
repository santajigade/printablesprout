This page has a friendly reindeer sitting in the snow, wearing a striped scarf, with two big branching antlers. Pine trees stand on each side, snowflakes fall, and four clumps of lichen sit on the ground, which is the food real reindeer dig for in winter. Type a name and the title becomes "Mia's Reindeer".

## Two versions

- **With antlers:** the full reindeer, ready to color, with antler points to count.
- **Draw the antlers:** the same reindeer with an empty space above its head. Kids draw their own antlers with the crayon tool or a pencil, as big and branchy as they like.

Choose a version above the sheet before you print or color online.

## A five minute antler counting game

Antlers are a natural counting tool because every point sticks out clearly. Try this before your child starts coloring:

1. **Count one side.** Put a finger on each point of the left antler and count out loud. There are 4.
2. **Count the other side.** The right antler is a mirror image, so it has 4 too.
3. **Add them up.** Ask how many points the reindeer has in all. The answer is 8.
4. **Color by count.** Color the first point on each antler one color, the second point another color, and so on.
5. **Switch versions.** Print the Draw the antlers version and challenge your child to draw an antler with 5 points, then count again.

For three and four year olds, stop at step 1 and just count to 4 together.

## Real reindeer facts that match the picture

Kids often ask whether reindeer are real. They are, and a few details on this page come straight from how they live:

- **Reindeer and caribou are the same animal.** The <a href="https://www.usgs.gov/index.php/news/national-news-release/other-364-days-year-real-lives-wild-reindeer" target="_blank" rel="noopener">U.S. Geological Survey</a> explains that "reindeer" is the name used in Europe and Asia and "caribou" is the North American name for the same species. The National Park Service describes reindeer as domestic caribou.
- **Girls grow antlers too.** Caribou are the only member of the deer family where both males and females grow antlers. The <a href="https://www.nps.gov/articles/caribou-did-you-know.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Park Service</a> notes that males drop their antlers in late fall, while females can keep theirs all winter. So a reindeer still wearing antlers in the middle of winter may well be a female.
- **The lumps on the ground are lunch.** In winter, lichens make up most of a reindeer's diet in most areas. One kind that grows on the tundra is sometimes called reindeer moss.
- **Big hooves are snowshoes.** Wide hooves help reindeer walk on snow and dig through it to reach food. Caribou can even swim, and their hollow hair helps them float.
- **Fur almost everywhere.** A reindeer's thick coat covers its whole body except the very tip of its nose.

Ask your child to point to the antlers, hooves and lichen on the page as you share each fact.
