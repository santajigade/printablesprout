Bedtime is easier when the steps are the same every night and your child can see them. This chart has six picture steps, from bath to lights out, and a circle for each night of the week. Your child colors a circle as each step gets done.

## Why the order matters

The American Academy of Pediatrics explains that <a href="https://healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Brush-Book-Bed.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">a predictable nighttime routine helps children learn what comes next</a>, and its Brush, Book, Bed approach puts brushing teeth before story time. This chart follows that order: bath, pajamas, teeth, story, hugs, then lights out. Once the teeth are brushed, the calm part of the night begins.

## Steps on the chart

1. Bath time
2. Pajamas on
3. Brush my teeth
4. Story time
5. Hugs and goodnight
6. Lights out

Retype any step in the boxes next to the chart. Families who bathe in the morning might change step 1 to "Tidy my room". The American Academy of Pediatrics notes that for school-aged children, <a href="https://healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/Bedtime-Routines-for-School-Aged-Children.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">a quick tidy-up is part of the bedtime routine</a>, so that swap works well for older kids.

## Making bedtime calmer with the chart

- **Start the chart at the same time each night.** Write your lights out time at the bottom so everyone knows the plan.
- **Let your child color the circle.** Handing over the crayon gives them a small job and a reason to move to the next step.
- **Keep story time last before goodnight.** It gives your child something to look forward to.
- **Use the chart instead of reminders.** "What does the chart say is next?" invites cooperation without a battle.

For toddlers who resist bedtime, the AAP suggests <a href="https://healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/Bedtime-Trouble.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">setting up a quiet routine before bed</a> so they understand that sleep is coming. A picture chart makes that routine visible even before a child can read.

## Setting it up by age

| Age | Tip |
|---|---|
| 2 to 3 | Use pictures only. Say each step out loud and color the circle together |
| 4 to 5 | Your child colors the circles. Offer a choice of two books at story time |
| 6 to 8 | Hand over steps 1 to 3. As the AAP notes, step back gradually as they take charge |

## When the routine slips

Busy nights happen. If a step gets skipped, do not erase anything: simply leave that circle empty and start fresh the next night. A chart that shows a few gaps is still doing its job, because it keeps the order of the steps the same.
