A reward chart turns one goal into a path your child can see. This chart has 20 numbered stars that wind back and forth like a board game, a goal line at the top and a prize at the end. Type your child's name, the goal and the prize, then print.

## Set it up in three steps

1. **Pick one goal.** Something specific and repeatable works best, like "Brush my teeth every night" or "Stay in bed after lights out".
2. **Pick a prize your child cares about.** Experiences often beat things: choosing a family movie, a trip to the library, or baking together.
3. **Print and hang it** somewhere your child passes every day, such as the fridge or bedroom door.

Each time the goal is met, your child colors or stickers the next star, starting at 1.

## Why a path of 20 stars

Kids can see exactly how far they have come and how far is left, which a plain grid does not show as clearly. Twenty steps is long enough to build a habit over a few weeks but short enough that the prize still feels close.

| Goal type | Suggested pace | Stars to prize |
|---|---|---|
| Daily habit (teeth, bedtime) | One star per day | 20, about three weeks |
| New skill (tying shoes, reading) | One star per practice | 20 |
| Younger child, 3 to 4 | One star per day | 10, then a new chart |

## Getting the most out of it

The CDC recommends that parents <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/using-rewards.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">praise good behavior with specific, positive words</a> and use simple reward charts to track progress, adjusting goals and rewards if the system is not working. A few habits make that easier:

- **Give the star right away.** A star earned at bedtime should be colored at bedtime, not the next morning.
- **Say what earned it.** "You brushed for the whole song" connects the star to the action.
- **Never take stars away.** The chart only moves forward. Missed days simply do not get a star.
- **Change the goal if it stalls.** If a week passes with no stars, the goal may be too big. Break it into a smaller step.

The CDC's <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/preschooler-3-5-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">positive parenting tips for preschoolers</a> also suggest giving young children simple choices, so let your child help pick the prize.

## When the chart is full

Celebrate the finished chart, then let your child keep it. A new chart can start the next day with the same goal or a new one.
