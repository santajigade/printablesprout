Midnight is a long way off for a five year old. This free printable turns New Year's Eve into a countdown kids can actually finish: six bag tags, each with a time, a picture to color and one small activity. Fill six paper bags, tie on the tags and open one bag at each time. The last tag is the big "10 to 1" countdown, set early enough that little ones are still awake for it.

New Year's Day 2027 is a Friday, according to the <a href="https://www.wieb.uscourts.gov/node/28" target="_blank" rel="noopener">federal court holiday schedule</a>, so the countdown night is **Thursday, December 31, 2026**.

## Two versions on one sheet

| Version | Times printed on the tags | Final countdown |
|---|---|---|
| Evening countdown | 6:00, 7:00, 7:30, 8:00, 8:30 and 9:00 PM | 9:00 PM |
| Noon Year's Eve | 9:00, 9:30, 10:00, 10:30 and 11:15 AM, then 12:00 PM | Noon |

Every time is editable. Type your own times before printing and they replace the printed ones, so a family with a late bedtime can push the whole plan back, and a family with toddlers can space the tags closer together. Type a name and the title becomes "Sam's New Year's Countdown". The pictures on the tags (party hat, music note, cookie, dice, star, clock, pancakes and balloon) are fillable, so kids can color the tags on a tablet or with crayons before cutting them out.

## Picking a start time from your child's bedtime

The best final countdown time is close to your child's normal bedtime. The <a href="https://sleepeducation.org/how-much-sleep-children-need/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">American Academy of Sleep Medicine</a> recommends 10 to 13 hours of sleep in 24 hours for children 3 to 5 years old, including naps, and 9 to 12 hours for children 6 to 12. The American Academy of Pediatrics says on <a href="https://healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/Healthy-Sleep-Habits-How-Many-Hours-Does-Your-Child-Need.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">HealthyChildren.org</a> that it supports these guidelines. If tomorrow's wake-up time stays the same, a very late night cuts into those hours.

A simple way to set the times:

1. Write down your child's usual bedtime. Make that the time on tag 6.
2. Count back 30 minutes for tag 5, then 30 more for tag 4, and so on.
3. If that puts tag 1 too early, use 20 minute gaps instead.
4. For toddlers or a family that wants a calm night, print the Noon Year's Eve version and do the whole party before lunch.

## What to put in each bag

Use things you already have. Here is a plan that matches the activity on each tag:

| Tag | Activity | Put in the bag |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make a party hat | A sheet of paper, tape, stickers or crayons |
| 2 | Dance party | A note with three song names to play |
| 3 | Floor picnic snack (or pancakes at noon) | A small snack or a picnic napkin |
| 4 | Family game time (or balloon keep-up) | A deck of cards, a simple game or an uninflated balloon |
| 5 | Draw a wish for 2027 | A blank card and a pencil |
| 6 | Count down 10 to 1 | Noisemakers, pot lids and spoons, or paper confetti |

Number the bags 1 to 6 and line them up on a table where kids can see them all evening. Seeing the row of bags get shorter is half the fun, and it answers the question "how long until the countdown?" without a clock.

## Making the most of tag 5

The wish card is the one families keep. Ask each child to draw one thing they want to do in 2027 and one thing they loved about 2026. Date the card and tuck it into a keepsake box.
