A snowman is a winter classic that works from the first cold day to the last. This page has a three-ball snowman with a top hat, a striped scarf, a carrot nose, stick arms and buttons, standing on a snowy hill under six snowflakes. Type a name and the title becomes "Zoe's Snowman".

## What kids can color

- The top hat and its band
- A long scarf with stripes
- The carrot nose and five buttons
- The snowy hill and the sky behind it
- Six snowflakes of two sizes

Because the snowman is mostly white, the fun is in the details. Encourage kids to color the scarf in stripes and the sky in a deep blue so the snowman stands out.

## A quick snowflake science moment

Before coloring, look closely at the snowflakes on the page and count their arms. Every one has six. That is not just our drawing style: NOAA explains that <a href="https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/k-12-education/ice-snow/how-do-snowflakes-form" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the crystal structure of frozen ice is six-sided</a>, which gives snowflakes their symmetry. Older kids can try NOAA's <a href="https://scijinks.gov/snow-crystals" target="_blank" rel="noopener">snowflake simulator</a> to see how temperature changes a crystal's shape, then come back and draw their own six-armed flakes with the crayon tool.

## Winter day activity plan

| Step | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Count the snowflakes and their arms | 2 minutes |
| 2 | Color the snowman's hat, scarf and nose | 10 minutes |
| 3 | Color the sky and hill | 5 minutes |
| 4 | Draw three new snowflakes with six arms each | 5 minutes |

## Tips for younger colorers

- Start with the big spaces: the hill, the sky and the hat.
- Use tap to fill on a tablet for the scarf stripes, which are small for little hands.
- Leave the snowman white, or try light blue for a shadow effect.

## Printing well

The page prints as black line art on US Letter or A4. For a classroom winter display, print on light blue paper and have kids color only the hat, scarf and nose.

## Using it in a classroom or group

The snowman works well as a calm activity after recess or before winter break. Some easy ways to use it with a group:

- **Name the snowman.** Each child types or writes a name for their snowman in the title, which makes every sheet different.
- **Make a snowman wall.** Hang the finished pages side by side. Kids love spotting the different scarves and hats.
- **Add a writing line.** On the back, older children can write two sentences about what their snowman would do on a snow day.
- **Talk about shapes.** The snowman is built from three circles of different sizes, which makes it a simple way to talk about big, medium and small with preschoolers.
