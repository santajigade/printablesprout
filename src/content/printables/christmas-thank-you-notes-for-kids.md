After the gifts are opened, a thank you note is one of the easiest ways for kids to practice gratitude. This free kids thank you notes printable puts **two notes on one page**, so you cut once and have two cards ready to mail or hand over. Each note has a big "Thank You!" title, a gift, star and heart to color, and a box for a picture.

There are two versions. **Fill in the blank** walks kids through a simple note: "Dear ___, Thank you for ___. I like it because ___. Love, ___". **Draw your thanks** keeps just the greeting and the gift line and gives the rest of the note to a big drawing box, which suits kids who are not writing yet. Type names and gifts before printing, or leave the lines empty for kids to fill in by hand. Christmas is Friday, December 25, so printing a few sheets now means they are ready when the wrapping paper is gone.

## A three step note formula kids can remember

The fill in the blank lines follow a pattern that grown-ups can teach out loud:

1. **Who it is for.** "Dear Grandma,"
2. **What you are thankful for.** Name the gift or the kind act.
3. **Why you like it.** One specific detail, such as "it has rockets on it" or "we built it together."

That third line matters most. Children's Hospital Colorado therapist Marisa Taylor encourages families to get "really specific about what we're thankful for," and a single real detail makes the note feel personal. The picture box adds a second way to say it: kids can draw themselves using the gift or playing with the person who gave it.

## Matching the note to your child's age

| Age | Version | What the child does | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 and 4 | Draw your thanks | Draws a picture and colors the heart | Type the names and gift, ask "What do you love about it?" and write their answer on the back |
| 5 and 6 | Draw your thanks or Fill in the blank | Copies the gift word, draws the picture | Spell words on a sticky note to copy |
| 7 and 8 | Fill in the blank | Writes every line by hand | Help with the "because" line if they get stuck |
| 9 and 10 | Fill in the blank | Writes the note, adds a second sentence on the back | Address and stamp the envelope together |

## Thank you notes without the nagging

The <a href="https://mcc.gse.harvard.edu/resources-for-families/help-kids-show-gratitude" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Making Caring Common project at Harvard</a> suggests letting kids show thanks in ways that feel right to them. A written note, a drawing or a short video can all count, and the project says to emphasize being genuine over being perfect. It also recommends mailing the card together so kids learn how, especially if they have never mailed a letter before, and it says adults should model the habit too.

The American Academy of Pediatrics makes a similar point in its <a href="https://healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/how-to-practice-gratitude.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">guide to nurturing gratitude in children</a>: be a role model, encourage sincere spoken or written thanks, and do not save gratitude talk for the holidays. A simple way to do all three is to sit down and write your own thank you notes at the same table while your child works on theirs.

A few practical tips:

- Make a short list of who gave what as gifts are opened, so nobody is forgotten.
- Do one sheet a day instead of every note at once.
- Let kids choose who gets each note and color it however they like.
- Snap a photo of each finished note before it goes in the mail.
