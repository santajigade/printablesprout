The 100th day of school is a big party in many kindergarten and first grade classrooms, and this free 100th day of school printable gives kids two ways to celebrate the number. The **Hundreds chart** version has every number from 1 to 100 in a 10 by 10 grid, with the tens column in bold. Both versions share a writing box at the bottom with three prompts: the date of the 100th day, "In 100 days I learned to" and the class favorite, "When I am 100 years old, I will". ## How to find your school's 100th day

There is no national date because school calendars differ by state and district. Pew Research Center found that 180 instructional days is the most common requirement: 27 of the 38 states with a day minimum, plus Washington, D.C., set it at 180. <a href="https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/how-much-time-do-students-spend-in-school-it-depends-on-the-state/2026/10" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Education Week's 2026 state comparison</a> puts the range at 160 days in Colorado up to 186 in Kansas, and notes that many states count hours instead of days.

So the quickest way to find day 100 is to count it yourself:

1. Get your district calendar and mark the first day of school.
2. Remember that 100 school days is 20 full five-day weeks.
3. Count 20 weeks forward from the first day.
4. Add one more school day for every weekday the school is closed: holidays, teacher work days and each weekday of a break.

**A worked example.** Say school started on Monday, August 17, 2026, with six single days off and a two-week winter break (ten weekdays) before New Year. Twenty weeks of school would end on Friday, January 1, 2027. Add the 16 closed weekdays (6 plus 10), which is three more weeks and one day, and the 100th day lands on Monday, January 25, 2027. Your own calendar will give a different date, but the steps are the same.

## Ways to play with the hundreds chart

The chart works best as a pattern hunt, not a coloring race. Some ideas that take five minutes each:

- **The tens column.** Color 10, 20, 30 and down to 100. Kids see that every number in that column ends in zero.
- **Count by fives.** Color 5, 10, 15 and so on in a second color. Two stripes appear.
- **Mystery number.** Give clues ("It is in the 40s and ends in 7") and let kids color the answer.
- **Day counter.** In the weeks before the 100th day, color one square for each school day so far. The sheet fills up just as the party arrives.
