This turkey turns the wait for dinner into a small gratitude activity: each of the seven feathers holds one thing your child is thankful for.

## Writing lines or pattern feathers

Use the **Version** buttons above the sheet to switch between two layouts before you print.

- **Writing lines** puts four dashed lines on every feather. Readers and early writers fill one feather with one thing they are thankful for.
- **Patterns** swaps the lines for a feather vein with stripes. This suits toddlers and preschoolers who want to color rather than write.

Both versions print as black line art, so they work on any home printer and use very little ink.

## A five minute gratitude round at the kids' table

This is the part most turkey printables skip: how to turn the sheet into a short, calm activity that kids actually finish.

1. Print one sheet per child and set them out with crayons before guests arrive.
2. Give everyone a prompt for each feather: a person, a pet, a food, a place, a toy, a day they remember and something about themselves.
3. While the turkey roasts, let kids write or draw one answer per feather.
4. Before dessert, go around the table and have each child read one feather out loud.
5. Write the date at the bottom and keep the sheet. Next year, make a new one and compare.

If you have mixed ages at the table, an older cousin can be the scribe for a younger child. That keeps the little ones involved without frustration.

## Matching the activity to your child's age

| Age | Version to print | What they can do |
|---|---|---|
| 3 to 5 | Patterns | Color, while an adult writes one word they say on each feather |
| 6 to 8 | Writing lines | One word or short phrase per feather |
| 9 and up | Writing lines | Full sentences, plus a reason for each answer |

For a classroom, print the blank version with no name and let each student write their own name on the title line.

## Coloring it on screen

Online coloring works on phones, tablets and computers. Tap to fill colors whole shapes, including the title letters and the background. Crayon mode draws freely, so kids can add patterns or write on the feathers with a finger. **Undo** removes the last step, and white works as an eraser for fills. When the turkey is finished, **Download picture** saves a sharp image you can send to grandparents.
