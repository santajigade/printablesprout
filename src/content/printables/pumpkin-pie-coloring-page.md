Pumpkin pie is the dessert most kids know from the Thanksgiving table, and it is also a perfect circle cut into equal slices. This coloring page uses that. The pie sits on a plate with a scalloped crust, a dollop of whipped cream on every slice, a fork, a pie server and three little pumpkins to color at the bottom. Type a name and the title reads "Ava's Pumpkin Pie".

## Three pies, three fraction levels

Choose a version above the sheet before you print. Each one is cut differently and labels every slice with its fraction.

| Version | Slices | Each slice is | Where it shows up in school math |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 halves | 2 | 1/2 | Grade 1: halves and fourths of circles |
| 4 slices | 4 | 1/4 | Grade 1 and Grade 2: fourths, also called quarters |
| 8 slices | 8 | 1/8 | Grade 3: fractions as numbers, eighths included |

The grade levels come from the Common Core math standards that many states use. In first grade, children <a href="https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/id/web/302" target="_blank" rel="noopener">split circles and rectangles into two and four equal shares</a> and learn the words halves, fourths and quarters. Second grade adds thirds, and third grade treats a fraction like 1/8 as one part of a whole cut into 8 equal parts. Your child's school may teach these in a different order, so treat the table as a rough guide.

## A ten minute fraction talk while the real pie cools

The sheet works best next to a real pie, or any round food you can slice. Here is a simple plan:

1. **Count first.** Ask how many slices are on the paper pie. Point to each label as you count.
2. **Color one slice.** Ask your child to color a single slice and say its name out loud: "one eighth".
3. **Try the challenge line.** Each version has a short task at the bottom, such as coloring 3 slices orange and finding how many eighths are left (the answer is 5 eighths).
4. **Compare sizes.** If you printed two versions, lay them side by side. Ask which is bigger, one fourth or one eighth. Many kids guess eighth because 8 is bigger than 4, and the pictures show why that guess is wrong.
5. **Cut the real pie.** Let your child watch the knife make halves, then fourths, then eighths, and match each step to the sheet.

## Color ideas that make the slices easy to see

- Give each slice a different color, like a rainbow pie.
- Color only the filling orange and leave the crust tan, so the slice lines stand out.
- Color the whipped cream on two slices and ask what fraction has cream.
- Use the crayon tool online to draw a slice on the empty plate rim and label it.

## Where pie pumpkins come from

While kids color the little pumpkins at the bottom of the page, share a farm fact. According to the <a href="https://ers.usda.gov/newsroom/trending-topics/pumpkins-background-statistics" target="_blank" rel="noopener">USDA Economic Research Service</a>, Illinois grew about 533 million pounds of pumpkins in 2025, more than the next four states put together. Most of that Illinois crop is grown for processing into foods like pie filling and puree. Across the country it is the other way around: most pumpkin acres go to fresh and decorative pumpkins, like the ones carved and set on porches.

## Coloring online or on paper

On a tablet, tap to fill colors each slice in one tap, which suits younger kids whose hands are not ready for small crayon work. Undo takes back a slice colored by mistake. On paper, the bold lines print cleanly on US Letter or A4 at 100 percent. Crayons work well for the big filling areas and colored pencils for the scalloped crust.
