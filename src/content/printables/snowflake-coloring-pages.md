Snowflakes are one of the few things in nature that come with built-in symmetry, which makes them great for coloring and for a little science. This set has three sheets in one: a big six-armed snowflake built from diamonds and hexagons with four smaller flakes around it, a science sheet showing three real snowflake shapes, and a blank template for designing your own. Type a name and the title becomes "Isla's Snowflakes".

## What is on each sheet

Use the **Version** buttons above the page to switch.

- **Big snowflake.** One large flake with six arms, each carrying a long spine, four diamond branches and a small hexagon at the tip. Four smaller flakes sit in the corners. Every piece is its own shape, so kids can color a pattern that repeats around all six arms.
- **Snowflake shapes.** Three boxes show a cluster of needle crystals, a flat six-sided plate and a lacy star, each with a short fact.
- **Draw your own.** A dashed hexagon with six guide lines and a center hexagon. Kids draw one arm on the top line, then copy it onto the other five.

## The science behind the six arms

Every snowflake on these sheets has six arms or six sides, and that is true to life. NOAA explains that <a href="https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/k-12-education/ice-snow/how-do-snowflakes-form" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the crystal structure of frozen ice is six-sided</a>, and scientists call the result six-fold radial symmetry. Draw six evenly spaced lines out from the center of a flake and whatever grows on one line repeats on the other five.

Here is how a flake grows, in kid-friendly steps drawn from NOAA's explainers:

1. A very cold water droplet freezes onto a tiny speck of pollen or dust high in a cloud.
2. Water vapor in the air freezes onto that first crystal, which usually starts as a small six-sided prism.
3. Flat faces grow, and bumps catch extra vapor and stretch into branches.
4. As the flake falls, it passes through air of different temperatures and moisture, and each change alters how the arms grow.
5. All six arms meet the same conditions at the same time, so they grow to look alike.

## Why the shapes sheet shows needles and plates

Not every snowflake is the lacy star from holiday cards. According to NOAA's <a href="https://www.noaa.gov/stories/how-do-snowflakes-form" target="_blank" rel="noopener">guide to the science behind snow</a>, temperature mostly decides a crystal's basic shape, with humidity playing a smaller part. Long needle-like crystals form near 23 degrees Fahrenheit, and very flat plates form near 5 degrees Fahrenheit. That is why the science sheet labels each shape with its temperature.

A good question to ask while coloring: "If it is 23 degrees in the cloud, which box would your snowflake be in?"

## Are any two snowflakes the same?

Probably not. Each flake takes its own path down through the cloud, and NOAA SciJinks says matching the exact same sequence of conditions down to the microscopic level would be <a href="https://scijinks.gov/snowflakes" target="_blank" rel="noopener">astronomically unlikely</a>. That makes a fun rule for the Draw your own sheet: if two kids design snowflakes, they should look different too.

## Pick a sheet by age

| Age | Best sheet | Try this |

| 4 to 5 | Big snowflake | Count the six arms, then color all the tips one color |
| 6 to 7 | Snowflake shapes | Match each shape to its name and color the plate in a ring pattern |
| 8 to 10 | Draw your own | Draw one arm with two branches, then copy it exactly five times |

For the Draw your own sheet on screen, switch to the crayon tool before drawing. On paper, a pencil first and crayon second makes it easier to fix a wobbly arm.

## Ways to display them

- **Window flakes.** Color the big snowflake in blues and purples, cut around the outside and tape it to a window.
- **A snowflake wall.** In a classroom, hang every Draw your own sheet side by side and let kids look for two that match. They will not find any.
- **Pattern challenge.** Color the big flake so the diamonds alternate two colors around each arm, then check that all six arms match.
