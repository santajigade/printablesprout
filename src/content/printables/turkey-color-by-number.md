This color by number turkey gives kids a picture that comes together one number at a time: fanned tail feathers, a round body, two pumpkins, falling leaves and a sky behind it all. Every space has a number, and the key at the bottom of the sheet says which color goes there.

## Pick the version that fits your child

Use the **Version** buttons above the sheet before you print or start coloring.

- **Numbers 1 to 6** shows a single number in every space. It suits children who know their numbers and can match a numeral to the key.
- **Addition sums** swaps each number for a small sum such as 2+2 or 4+1. Every answer is between 1 and 6, so kids solve the sum first, then look up the color.

Both versions use the same picture and the same key, which means siblings at different levels can color matching turkeys side by side.

## The color key, matched to the online palette

| Number | Color | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brown | Body and head |
| 2 | Orange | Every other outer feather, wings, feet, pumpkins |
| 3 | Gold | Inner feathers, belly, beak, two leaves |
| 4 | Red | Outer feathers, wattle, two leaves |
| 5 | Green | Grassy ground |
| 6 | Sky blue | The sky behind the turkey |

These six names match swatches in the online coloring palette, so a child on a tablet can find "Gold" or "Sky blue" by name. With crayons, yellow works fine for gold and any light blue works for the sky. The color key circles are fillable too: coloring them first gives kids a handy reference while they work.

## Turkey facts to share while the colors go on

Coloring is a good moment for a few true facts about the real bird. The <a href="https://blog.nwf.org/2011/11/twelve-unusual-and-fascinating-facts-about-wild-turkeys/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Wildlife Federation's wild turkey facts</a> and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service offer plenty:

- **A turkey's bare head can change color within seconds** when the bird is excited, shifting between red, white and blue. On this sheet the wattle is marked red, one of those three colors.
- **Wild turkeys are fast.** They can run about 25 miles per hour and fly in short bursts of up to 55 miles per hour.
- **They sleep in trees**, even though people mostly see them walking.
- **An adult wild turkey has 5,000 to 6,000 feathers.** Our turkey has 16 tail feathers, so ask your child to count them and compare.
- **Turkeys have special names.** Adult males are called toms or gobblers, adult females are hens and the babies are poults.

## Matching the sheet to each age

| Age | Best version | How to help |
|---|---|---|
| 3 to 4 | Numbers | Color the key together first, then hunt for every 1 before moving on to 2 |
| 5 to 6 | Numbers, then Sums | Try the addition version with fingers or small counters for each sum |
| 7 to 8 | Sums | Have them write the answer next to each sum before coloring |

Working one number at a time is easier than jumping around the page. It also keeps crayon swaps to six, which helps small hands stay organized.

## A slow morning plan for Thanksgiving Day

1. Print one copy of each version the night before.
2. While breakfast cooks, have kids color the key circles and the sky.
3. Save the feathers for later in the morning, when there is more waiting.
4. Once everyone is seated, let kids show their turkeys and explain one turkey fact they learned.
