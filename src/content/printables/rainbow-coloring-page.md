A rainbow is one of the first pictures kids learn to draw, and it is also a neat little science lesson. This free rainbow coloring page has three versions. **Letter guide** marks each of the seven bands with R, O, Y, G, B, I or V so kids can match the right crayon. **Plain rainbow** has no letters, for kids who want to color it from memory. **Double rainbow** shows a second, thinner bow on the outside with its colors in reverse order, which surprises almost every child who sees it.

Every version also has two rain clouds with drops, puffy clouds at the rainbow's feet, and a hill with flowers and a puddle. Type a name and the title turns into "Leo's Rainbow". Color it on screen with tap to fill, or print it on US Letter or A4.

## The letter code on the bands

| Letter | Color | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| R | Red | Outside edge (top) |
| O | Orange | Second band |
| Y | Yellow | Third band |
| G | Green | Middle band |
| B | Blue | Fifth band |
| I | Indigo | Sixth band, a deep blue purple |
| V | Violet | Inside edge (bottom) |

Read the letters top to bottom and you get ROY G BIV, the name kids use to remember the order. The <a href="https://scied.ucar.edu/kids/sky-wonders/rainbows" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UCAR Center for Science Education</a> lists the same seven colors and the same memory trick. NOAA's SciJinks puts it simply: violet is on the bottom and red is on the top.

If your crayon box has no indigo, a dark blue or a blue purple works well. The Met Office points out that Newton is said to have seen only five colors and added orange and indigo himself, so there is room for a little creativity here.

## Why we left the sun out of the picture

Kids often draw a sun right next to a rainbow, but the real sky does not work that way. According to <a href="https://scijinks.gov/rainbow" target="_blank" rel="noopener">NOAA's SciJinks rainbow explainer</a>, you only see a rainbow when the sun is behind you and there are water droplets in front of you. Sunlight goes into each drop, bends a little, bounces off the back of the drop and comes out split into colors. That is why rainbows often show up right after it rains, when the sun comes out behind you.

Ask your child: "If the rainbow is in front of us, where is the sun?" Then point at their back. It is a small question that sticks.

Two more facts from NOAA that kids enjoy:

- **A rainbow is really a circle.** From the ground we only see the top part. From an airplane, people can sometimes see the whole ring.
- **A double rainbow flips.** The second bow forms when light bounces twice inside the drops. It is fainter, and its order is reversed, with red on the bottom and violet on top. Our Double rainbow version shows this: look at the letters on the thin outer bow.

## Make a real rainbow after coloring

You do not need to wait for rain. UCAR suggests a garden hose or sprinkler on a sunny day: stand with the sun behind you and spray a fine mist in front of you. Then hold the colored sheet next to it and check the order together.
