School mornings go smoother when kids know what comes next without being told. This chart shows six morning steps with a picture for each, plus a circle for every school day from Monday to Friday. Your child colors a circle as they finish each step.

## The six steps on the chart

1. Get out of bed
2. Brush my teeth
3. Get dressed
4. Eat breakfast
5. Put on my shoes
6. Grab my backpack

Each step can be retyped in the boxes next to the chart before printing. Type your child's name to turn the title into "Ava's Morning Routine". At the bottom, write the time you need to be out the door.

## Getting a new routine to stick

A chart on the wall does not run the morning by itself. These steps help it become your child's routine rather than yours:

- **Walk through it the night before.** Read each step together and point at the pictures.
- **Hang it where the action is.** A bedroom door or bathroom mirror works better than the fridge.
- **Lay out clothes and the backpack at night.** Steps 3 and 6 then take seconds.
- **Point instead of talk.** When your child stalls, point at the next picture rather than repeating instructions.
- **Notice the finish.** A quick high five when all six circles are colored matters more than any prize.

## Adjusting for different ages

| Age | What changes |
|---|---|
| 3 to 4 | Keep three or four steps. Retype the rest to "Hug goodbye" or leave them as a fun extra |
| 5 to 6 | Use all six steps. Color the circles together at first |
| 7 to 8 | Let your child color circles alone and add a time goal at the bottom |

The CDC's <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/preschooler-3-5-years.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">positive parenting tips for preschoolers</a> suggest offering a limited number of simple choices, such as deciding what to wear. Building one choice into step 3 gives your child some control over the routine. As children get older, the American Academy of Pediatrics notes that <a href="https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Chores-and-Responsibility.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">kids can take on more responsibilities</a>, so add a step like packing a snack when your child is ready.

## Paper or screen

Print one chart per week, or keep it on a tablet and tap each circle to color it. The paper version is easier to see across a room, which helps on busy mornings.
