This coloring page gives kids a big, simple Christmas tree to decorate: three layers of branches, a star on top, twelve round ornaments, a dotted garland and four gifts underneath. Type a name and the title becomes "Ethan's Christmas Tree".

## What is on the page

- A three-layer tree with large, easy spaces for young colorers
- Twelve ornaments, each a separate shape for tap to fill
- A star topper and a dotted garland
- Four wrapped gifts with bows and ribbons
- Four snowflakes in the background

All lines are bold enough for crayons and print cleanly on US Letter or A4.

## Decorating ideas by age

| Age | Try this |
|---|---|
| 3 to 4 | Color the tree green and pick one color for every ornament |
| 5 to 6 | Give each ornament a different color and draw a pattern on the gifts |
| 7 to 8 | Add your own lights with the crayon tool and write a name tag on each gift |

## Turn it into a counting game

For preschoolers, the page doubles as a counting sheet. Ask them to count the ornaments, then color them in a pattern such as red, gold, red, gold. Count the gifts and the snowflakes too. Snowflakes are a fun science moment: NOAA explains that <a href="https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/k-12-education/ice-snow/how-do-snowflakes-form" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ice crystals are six-sided, which is where a snowflake's symmetry comes from</a>. Have your child count the six arms on each snowflake on this page.

## A bit of tree history to share

If your child asks where the big decorated trees come from, here is one answer: the National Christmas Tree near the White House has been lit every year since 1923, when President Calvin Coolidge lit the very first one, according to the <a href="https://thenationaltree.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2020_NCTL_Program.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Christmas Tree Lighting program</a>.

## Coloring on screen

Online, tap the tree, any ornament, the star or a gift to fill it with the chosen color. The background is fillable too, for a night sky or a snowy scene. Switch to Crayon to add tinsel or lights. When finished, **Download picture** saves it to share with family, or **Print** puts the colored version on paper.

## Using it with a group

At a classroom party or a family gathering, print one tree per child and set out a shared cup of crayons. Ask everyone to decorate their tree with a different theme, such as all one color, stripes on every ornament, or a snowy tree. When the trees are done, line them up and let the children guess each other's theme. It is a quiet, low mess activity that works in about 20 minutes.
