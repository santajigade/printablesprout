A wish list is often the first real letter a child writes, and it is a lot more fun when there is space to draw. This sheet has two versions. **Picture boxes** gives four big numbered boxes so pre-writers can draw what they hope for, with a line under each for the word. **Writing lines** has six numbered lines for kids who can spell their wishes. Both end with a line for a gift your child wants to *give*, a line for something they are proud of this year, and a "Love," signature, plus gifts, ornaments, a stocking and a tree to color along the bottom.

Type a name and the title becomes "Mia's Christmas Wish List". You can also change the greeting, so the same sheet works for Santa, a grandparent or a gift exchange. Christmas is Friday, December 25, so a list written this week leaves plenty of time to mail it or tuck it under the tree.

## Mailing the list to the North Pole

If your child wants Santa to get the letter, the U.S. Postal Service runs a real program for it. USPS Operation Santa is in its 114th year, and the Postal Service's <a href="https://about.usps.com/newsroom/national-releases/2026/0914-usps-operation-santa-program-now-accepting-letters.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">2026 Operation Santa announcement</a> gives these details:

| What | Detail for 2026 |
|---|---|
| Address | Santa Claus, 123 Elf Rd., North Pole, 88888 |
| Deadline | Postmarked by December 5 to be eligible for adoption |
| Who can send | Children and families in the continental U.S., Hawaii and Puerto Rico |
| Adoption | Volunteers could adopt letters starting November 16, but adoption is not guaranteed |
| Rules | Letters must follow the program guidelines on USPSOperationSanta.com |

Because adoption is not guaranteed, it helps to tell your child that writing the letter is the fun part, and that the grown-ups at home will read a copy too. Print two sheets: one to mail and one to keep.

## How to fill it in, by age

| Age | Best version | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| 3 and 4 | Picture boxes | Your child draws, you write the word on the line. One or two wishes is plenty. |
| 5 and 6 | Picture boxes | Your child draws and copies the word from a sticky note you write first. |
| 7 and 8 | Writing lines | Ask for wishes in order of how much they want them, number 1 first. |
| 9 and 10 | Writing lines | Ask them to add a detail, such as a size or a color, so the list is useful to the adults. |

The "gift I would like to give" line turns a want list into a giving list. Kids can name a person and a small idea, like a drawing for Grandpa or helping a sibling wrap a present. The "proud of this year" line is a nice record to keep: put this year's sheet in a folder and compare next December.

## A quick toy safety check for the grown-ups

Once the list is done, it is worth reading it with the toy labels in mind. The <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Toys" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CPSC toy safety guidance</a> gives a short checklist for gifts:

- **Follow the age guidance on the package** and pick toys that suit the child's interests and abilities. Toys that are a choking hazard for children under 3 must carry an age label.
- **Keep small parts and small balls away from children under 3**, and keep deflated balloons away from children under 8. This matters most when an older sibling's wish list includes a small-part toy.
- **Pair riding toys with safety gear.** If a scooter is on the list, add a helmet and make sure it is worn every time. In its holiday safety release, the CPSC reported that non-motorized scooters caused about 35,400 emergency room treated toy injuries in 2022, the largest share of any toy.
- **Throw away plastic wrapping** right after gifts are opened.
