"How many more days?" is the most common question in December. This countdown answers it on paper: 25 boxes, one for each day from December 1 to December 25, with an ornament to color every morning and a tree waiting on day 25. Christmas Day falls on Friday, December 25 this year, according to the <a href="https://www.wiwb.uscourts.gov/node/28" target="_blank" rel="noopener">2026 federal holiday schedule</a>.

## How it works

1. Type your child's name so the title reads "Mia's Christmas Countdown".
2. Print it before December 1 and hang it on the fridge or a bedroom door.
3. Each day, your child colors the ornament for that date.
4. On December 25, they color the tree in the last box.

The ornaments come in three designs, plain, wavy and dotted, so coloring stays interesting through the month.

## A tiny daily tradition

The countdown takes about two minutes a day, which makes it easy to keep up through a busy month. A few ideas make it feel special:

- **Pick a fixed time,** such as after breakfast, so it becomes part of the routine.
- **Pair each box with a small family moment,** like a carol, a holiday book or a phone call to a grandparent. Write the activity inside the box with a pencil.
- **Count the empty boxes together.** It turns the waiting into a quick math game for younger kids.
- **Take a photo on the 25th.** The finished sheet is a nice keepsake to compare year to year.

## Activity ideas to write in the boxes

| Days | Idea for the box |
|---|---|
| 1 to 7 | Make a paper snowflake, read a winter story, help decorate |
| 8 to 14 | Bake or decorate cookies, make a card for a neighbor, sing a carol |
| 15 to 21 | Look at holiday lights, donate a toy, watch a family movie |
| 22 to 24 | Wrap a gift, color the tree page, set out a winter picnic |

Holiday lights are a long tradition in the United States: the first National Christmas Tree lighting at President's Park took place in 1923, according to the <a href="https://thenationaltree.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2020_NCTL_Program.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Christmas Tree Lighting program</a>. A lights walk in your own neighborhood makes a good activity for one of the boxes.

## Coloring it on screen

On a tablet, tap each ornament to fill it with a color. Because colors are not saved between visits, the paper version is better for a month-long countdown, but the screen version is handy for a quick look or for coloring several days at once.
