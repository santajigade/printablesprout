Winter turns an ordinary walk into something to explore: bare branches, birds at the feeder, tracks in mud or snow. This scavenger hunt gives kids twelve things to look for, each with a picture so even pre-readers can play. When it is too cold or wet to go out, switch to the indoor version and hunt for cozy things around the house instead.

## Two hunts on one sheet

Choose a version with the **Version** buttons above the sheet.

**Outdoor hunt:** a pinecone, an evergreen tree, a bare tree, a bird, animal tracks, a long stick, a smooth rock, a brown leaf, a cloud, an icicle, a snowflake and a snowman.

**Indoor hunt:** a mitten, a warm hat, a scarf, snow boots, cozy socks, a winter coat, a blanket, a mug, a book, a pillow, a window and a puzzle piece.

Each square has a small circle in the corner. Kids color the circle when they find the item, and the hunt is done when all twelve circles are filled.

## No snow? Make the bottom row a bonus

Plenty of families have winters with no snow at all. On the outdoor sheet, only the icicle, snowflake and snowman need it, so those three squares sit together in the bottom row with a dashed border. In a mild climate, count them as bonus squares and call the hunt finished at nine. In snowy places, the bonus row tends to be the favorite.

## A warm clothes check before you head out

Run through this quick list at the door. It is built from the <a href="https://healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Winter-Safety.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">American Academy of Pediatrics winter safety tips</a> and the <a href="https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-during" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Weather Service cold safety guidance</a>.

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Several thin layers | The AAP says thin layers keep kids warm and dry |
| Hat on | Both sources list a warm hat for cold days |
| Mittens instead of gloves | The Weather Service says mittens are warmer than gloves |
| Insulated boots | The AAP lists toes, fingers, ears and noses as the spots most likely to get frostbite |
| A time limit and a warm-up spot | The AAP suggests limits on outdoor time with regular indoor breaks |

Two more points from the same sources are worth knowing. The AAP advises against outdoor play when the temperature or wind chill is below minus 15 degrees Fahrenheit, and kids should change out of wet clothes right away once they are inside. Children often do not come in on their own when they get too cold, so an adult keeps an eye on the clock. Redness or pain in the skin can be an early sign of frostbite, according to the Weather Service, which is a good reason to head in and warm up.

## How to run the hunt at each age

| Age | How to play | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| 3 to 4 | Walk together and point out each item | Hunt for three or four squares, not all twelve |
| 5 to 6 | Kids find items, an adult checks | Bring the sheet on a clipboard and a crayon in a pocket |
| 7 to 9 | Work in pairs or race a sibling | Add a rule: draw one extra thing you found in the margin |

## Making the indoor hunt last longer

- **Hide a few items first.** Tuck a mitten under a pillow and a book behind a curtain before kids start.
- **Add a timer.** Older kids like to see how fast they can fill every circle.
- **Swap roles.** When all twelve are found, the child hides something for a grown-up to find, just like the line at the bottom of the sheet says.
