Dinosaurs are one of the most requested coloring themes for young kids, and long-necked ones are a favorite. This page shows a friendly long-neck dinosaur reaching toward a palm tree, with a volcano in the distance, a smiling sun and big spots on its back. Type a name and the title becomes "Jack's Dinosaur".

## What is on the page

- A long-neck dinosaur with five round spots to color
- A tall palm tree with five fronds
- A small volcano in the background
- A sun with rays and a grassy ground line

The shapes are big and rounded, which suits preschoolers who are still learning to stay inside the lines.

## Dino facts to share while coloring

Long-necked dinosaurs belong to a group called sauropods. The American Museum of Natural History notes that <a href="https://www.amnh.org/explore/news-blogs/news-posts/know-your-sauropod" target="_blank" rel="noopener">long necks were key to sauropod gigantism</a>, letting them reach food without moving their huge bodies. According to the Cleveland Museum of Natural History, <a href="https://www.cmnh.org/exhibits/g3-4-302" target="_blank" rel="noopener">sauropods are the largest known animals ever to have walked on land</a>, and some grew longer than 30 meters, or about 100 feet.

A fun way to picture that with kids: 100 feet is roughly the length of a long hallway in a school. Ask your child to count big steps across a room and guess how many it would take to walk past a whole sauropod.

## Coloring online

On a phone or tablet, tap the body, neck, spots, palm fronds, volcano, sun or ground to fill each one. The crayon tool adds stripes, scales or a second dinosaur. Press **Download picture** to save the finished dinosaur, or **Print** to put it on the fridge.

## Turn it into a dinosaur story

Coloring is a great warm up for a short story. Once the page is finished, ask your child three questions and write their answers on the back of the sheet:

1. **What is your dinosaur's name?** Type it in the name box too, so it appears in the title.
2. **What does it like to eat?** Long-necked dinosaurs ate plants, so leaves from the palm tree are a good start.
3. **Where does it live?** Near the volcano, by a river or somewhere your child invents.

Younger children can say the answers out loud while you write. Older kids can turn the three answers into a short paragraph, which builds early writing skills without feeling like homework.
