A chore chart works best when the jobs fit the child and the progress is easy to see. This chart lets you type your child's name, up to six chores and a weekly reward right on the sheet, then print it. Every finished job earns a colored star in that day's box.

## How to fill it in

1. Type your child's name. The title becomes "Maya's Chore Chart".
2. Replace any of the six chores with jobs that fit your home. Short labels of two or three words fit best.
3. Add a reward in the last box, such as a park trip or picking dinner.
4. Print one chart per child per week and hang it at their eye level.

Stars can be colored by hand on paper, or on screen with tap to fill if you would rather keep the chart on a tablet.

## Picking chores that fit the age

The American Academy of Pediatrics notes that <a href="https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Chores-and-Responsibility.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">age-appropriate chores are an important part of a child's development</a>, and it warns against overloading kids. Starting small matters more than starting big. Use the table below as a starting point, then adjust for your child.

| Age | Two or three starter chores for the chart |
|---|---|
| 4 to 5 | Put toys away, put clothes in the hamper, help feed the pet |
| 6 to 7 | Make my bed, clear my plate, water the plants |
| 8 to 9 | Set the table, sort clean socks, pack my school bag |
| 10 to 12 | Load the dishwasher, take out the recycling, fold my laundry |

Six chores is the maximum on this chart, not the target. Younger children often do better with two or three, with the other lines left as our suggestions or covered with a sticker.

## Making the stars work

The CDC suggests <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/using-rewards.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">using simple reward charts to track behavior</a>, praising with specific words and adjusting goals if the system is not working. On this chart that looks like:

- **Name the job when you praise.** "You fed the dog without a reminder" says more than "good job".
- **Color the star together.** The moment of coloring is the small reward that keeps kids going.
- **Keep the weekly reward simple.** Time together, like a walk or a game, works as well as a treat.
- **Change one thing at a time.** If a chore never gets done, swap it next week rather than adding more rules.

## Siblings and shared jobs

Print a separate chart for each child, even when they share a chore like feeding a pet. Kids compare stars, and separate charts keep the focus on their own progress.
