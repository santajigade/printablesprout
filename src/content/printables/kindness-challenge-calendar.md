Kindness is easier to practice when it is small, specific and on the fridge where everyone can see it. This calendar has 30 boxes, each with a numbered heart and one short kind act underneath, from "Smile and say hi to someone" on day 1 to "Repeat your favorite act" on day 30. Your child does the act, then colors that day's heart. Type a name for "Ava's Kindness Challenge" and fill in the start date at the bottom so the finished sheet becomes a keepsake.

## How the 30 days build up

The acts are ordered on purpose, so the challenge gets a little bigger as your child gets better at it.

| Days | What kind of acts | Examples from the sheet |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 10 | Quick acts at home and with friends, most take a minute | Share a toy, help set the table, hold the door |
| 11 to 20 | Reaching people outside the family | Thank a teacher, wave to the mail carrier, call a relative |
| 21 to 30 | Acts that take courage or planning | Say sorry and make it right, sit with someone who is alone, plan a family surprise |

That shape follows two ideas from Harvard's <a href="https://mcc.gse.harvard.edu/resources-for-families/7-tips-raising-caring-kids" target="_blank" rel="noopener">tips for raising caring kids</a>. Caring is described there as a skill that grows with daily practice and gradually harder challenges, much like learning a sport or a language. The same project also encourages parents to widen a child's "circle of concern" beyond family and close friends, which is why the middle ten days look outward to neighbors, teachers and helpers.

## Five minutes of talk that make it stick

Doing the act is half of it. A short chat at dinner or bedtime turns it into a habit. Try one question per week:

- **Week 1:** How did the person look when you did your kind act?
- **Week 2:** Who helps our family that we never really thank?
- **Week 3:** What would make you feel welcome on your first day in a new class?
- **Week 4:** Was any act hard to do? What made it hard?

The week 3 question comes from the AAP's article on <a href="https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/Kindness-Can-Make-a-Big-Difference.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">why kindness makes a big difference</a>, which suggests asking kids to picture being the new student. Answers like someone to eat lunch with, help finding the classroom, or a smile and a hello lead straight into days 10 and 24 on the sheet.

## Grown-ups go first

Kids learn kindness mostly by watching. The AAP article points out that children notice how adults talk about people who are different from them, and Harvard's empathy tips note that kids watch how adults treat people such as servers and mail carriers. A few ways to model it during the challenge:

1. **Do the day's act too**, and say out loud what you did.
2. **Do it with your child, not just for them.** Making Caring Common prefers "doing with" others over only "doing for" them, so a card for a neighbor can be made and delivered together.
3. **Admit your own slips.** Day 23 is "Say sorry and make it right". It lands better if your child has heard you apologize.

## Matching the sheet to your child's age

| Age | How to run it |
|---|---|
| 4 and 5 | Read the act aloud each morning and do it together. Let them color the heart right after. |
| 6 and 7 | Let them read the act and plan when to do it. Help with notes and cards. |
| 8 to 10 | Let them run it alone, and add one sentence on the back about the hardest day. |

A Greater Good Science Center article on <a href="https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_to_be_a_kindness_role_model_for_your_kids" target="_blank" rel="noopener">being a kindness role model</a> describes a study of children aged 9 to 11 in which kids who regularly did acts of kindness became more accepted by their classmates, which is a nice point to share with older kids who wonder why they are doing this.
