Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26 in 2026, according to the <a href="https://www.wiwb.uscourts.gov/node/28" target="_blank" rel="noopener">federal holiday schedule</a>. This word search comes in two sizes on the same page, so a kindergartner and an older sibling can work on matching puzzles at the same table. Each grid has its own answer key, and every letter is large enough to circle with a finger on a tablet.

## Easy grid or hard grid

| Version | Grid | Words | Directions | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 8 by 8 | 8 short words | Across and down | Ages 5 to 7, new readers |
| Hard | 11 by 11 | 12 words, up to 9 letters | Across, down and diagonal | Ages 8 to 11 |

The easy list is TURKEY, PIE, CORN, FAMILY, PARADE, THANKS, GRAVY and FEAST. The hard list adds longer words such as GRATITUDE, CRANBERRY, STUFFING, HARVEST and AUTUMN. No word runs backwards in either grid, which keeps the puzzle fair for younger kids.

## Solving it on a phone or tablet

The page opens with the **Draw and write** tool, so kids can drag a finger around each word they find. A few extras make it work well on a screen:

- **Tap to fill a letter square** to highlight it instead of circling. This is easier for small children whose circles wander.
- **Mark the check box** next to a word in the list once it is found, with a tick or a tap of color.
- **Undo** removes the last mark if a circle lands on the wrong letters.
- **Switch to the answer key** under Version to check, then switch back.

## Why THURSDAY and NOVEMBER are hiding in the hard grid

Those two words carry a bit of history. The <a href="https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2023/11/20/thanksgiving-as-a-federal-holiday/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Archives explains how the date was settled</a>:

1. **1789:** President George Washington named Thursday, November 26 as a day of public thanksgiving.
2. **1863:** Abraham Lincoln's proclamation set the holiday on the last Thursday in November.
3. **1939:** Franklin Roosevelt moved it one week earlier, to the second to last Thursday.
4. **1941:** Congress fixed the holiday on the fourth Thursday of November. Roosevelt signed it on December 26, 1941, and it took effect the next year.

Here is a fun connection to share once the puzzle is done: this year's Thanksgiving lands on Thursday, November 26, the same date Washington picked in 1789.

## Word search tips for new readers

- **Hunt for the first letter.** Put a finger on every T in the grid, then check the letter to the right and below for a U.
- **Look for double letters.** STUFFING has two Fs in a row and CRANBERRY has two Rs, which stand out once you know to look for them.
- **Say the word out loud.** Sounding it out keeps the spelling in mind while the eyes scan.
- **Cross it off right away.** Coloring the check box stops kids from searching for the same word twice.

## Three ways to keep the words going

Once every word is found, the list can turn into a short game:

- **Sentence round:** each person picks a found word and uses it in a sentence about their own Thanksgiving.
- **Spelling challenge:** cover the list and ask kids to spell three words from memory.
- **Sort the words:** split the list into foods, people and calendar words, then talk about which group is biggest.
