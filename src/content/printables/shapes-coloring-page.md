Everything in Shape Town is made of shapes. The house is a square with a triangle roof and a round window, the tree is a circle on a rectangle trunk, the mountains are triangles, the kite is a square turned on its corner, and the town sign is a hexagon. There is even a cat with a circle face, triangle ears and a rectangle body. Kids color the town, then hunt for every circle, square, triangle, rectangle and hexagon hiding in it.

## Two ways to play in Shape Town

| Version | What kids do | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Shape hunt | Count each kind of shape and color the circle with the right number | Ages 3 to 5 |
| Color by shape | Color circles yellow, squares blue, triangles red, rectangles green and hexagons orange, then count all 32 shapes | Ages 4 to 6 |

Every shape is its own space, so kids can tap to fill on the online version or color on paper. The color key boxes are fillable too, so children can color the key first and check it as they go.

## Why the shapes are tilted, big and small

The five flags on the bunting hang upside down. The kite is a square standing on its point. One triangle mountain is tall and one is short. That is on purpose. Kindergarten standard <a href="https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/id/web/275" target="_blank" rel="noopener">K.G.2</a> asks children to name shapes correctly regardless of their orientation or overall size. If your child calls the kite a diamond, ask a great question: does turning a square make it a new shape?

The <a href="https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/id/web/274" target="_blank" rel="noopener">K.G.1 standard</a> lists the shapes kindergartners learn to identify and describe: squares, circles, triangles, rectangles and hexagons, plus the solid shapes cubes, cones, cylinders and spheres. It also asks children to describe where things are using words like above, below, beside, in front of, behind and next to. Shape Town gives you plenty to talk about: the sun is above the cloud, the cat is next to the stepping stones.

## Shape talk questions for the coloring time

Ask one or two while your child colors:

1. "How do you know that is a triangle?" (Look for three straight sides and three corners.)
2. "What is the difference between the door and the windows?" (The door is a long rectangle, the windows are squares.)
3. "Which shape has six sides?" (The sign and the stepping stones.)
4. "Can you find a circle that is round but tiny?" (The round window in the roof.)

The <a href="https://www.schools.utah.gov/curr/mathematics/_mathematics_/_core/_math_core_guides_tab_/CoreGuidesKindergartenGeometry.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Utah kindergarten geometry core guide</a> notes that circles are round while squares, triangles, rectangles and hexagons are made of straight sides, which is a simple way to sort the town into two groups.

## Build a shape after the page

The same guide includes composing shapes, such as joining two triangles to make a rectangle. Cut two paper triangles from scrap paper and let your child slide them together into a rectangle, then a bigger triangle. Then ask them to find a spot in Shape Town where a new shape could go and draw it in.
