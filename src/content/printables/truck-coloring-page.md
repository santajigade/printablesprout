This truck coloring page is a busy construction site with a job for every machine. An excavator scoops dirt with its bucket, a dump truck waits with a full load, and a bulldozer rolls past with its big blade, while a new building goes up in the background. After coloring, kids play **Match the job**: they color the DIG, PUSH and CARRY signs to match the machine that does each one. The **Site inspector** version turns the same picture into a hunt for five traffic cones and three hard hats.

## What is on the site

| Machine or item | Parts to color | Its job in the game |
|---|---|---|
| Excavator | Tracks, 4 wheels, cab, window, long arm, bucket | DIG |
| Bulldozer | Blade, body, cab, exhaust pipe, track with 5 wheels | PUSH |
| Dump truck | Cab, bed, load of dirt, 3 wheels with hubs | CARRY |
| Building | 9 windows and the frame | Background to color |
| Cones and hard hats | 5 cones, 3 hats | Site inspector hunt |

## How each machine really works

Kids who love trucks usually love knowing what each part does. These short explanations come straight from reference sources, so you can use them while your child colors.

**Bulldozer.** <a href="https://www.britannica.com/technology/bulldozer" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Encyclopaedia Britannica</a> describes a bulldozer as a powerful machine for pushing earth or rocks, with a heavy, broad steel blade on the front. It usually runs on metal tracks instead of wheels. The blade can be lowered below the ground to dig, held at the surface to move material, or raised a little to spread soil that dump trucks have dropped off. Bulldozers also clear tree stumps and boulders.

**Excavator.** Britannica groups excavating machines as machines used for digging or moving earth, and names the power shovel, the bulldozer and the grader as examples. Point out the long arm and bucket on the sheet and ask your child how the bucket would scoop the dirt pile.

**Dump truck.** On the sheet, the dump truck's bed is already heaped with dirt. Ask your child where the load should go next: to the bulldozer to spread, or away from the site.

## The safety rule printed on the sheet

The match the job version ends with a tip: big machines have blind spots, so wave from far away. It is based on research from the <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/motor-vehicle/constructionequipmentvisibilitydiagram/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CDC's National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health</a>, which maps blind areas, the spaces of limited visibility around construction vehicles, for dump trucks, dozers, excavators, loaders and more. NIOSH checks what the operator can see at ground level, at about 3 feet, and at the height of a person who is 4 feet 11 inches tall. If a driver can miss a grown person standing in one of those spaces, a child can be missed too, which is a good, concrete reason to keep a big distance from working machines.

A simple way to practice: next time you pass a work zone, ask your child to name each machine they see and where they think the driver's blind spots might be.

## Two levels of play

1. **Ages 3 to 5:** color the three big machines, then match the job signs with help reading the words.
2. **Ages 5 to 8:** switch to the Site inspector version. Find every cone and hard hat, color a box for each, and answer the bonus question about the blade.
3. **Extra challenge:** draw a fourth machine on the back, give it a job word for a new sign, and explain what it does.
