Kids change fast, and the small details disappear first: the favorite song they sang all December, the animal they wanted to be, how tall they were. This All About Me sheet catches those details on one page and turns them into a time capsule you open again a year later. New Year's Day falls on Friday, January 1, which makes winter break a natural time to fill it in.

## What is on the sheet

- **A "This is me!" frame** for a self portrait, with two stars on the corners to color.
- **Four quick facts** with circles to color as each is filled in: age, height, today's date, and grade or class.
- **Six favorites boxes** with pictures to color: food, color, animal, game or toy, book, and song.
- **Two reflection boxes**: the best thing about last year, and something to learn this year.
- **An "Open me again on" line** so the sheet knows when its time capsule is due.

Every line is editable on screen. Type a name and the title becomes "All About Leo", type the year and the subtitle reads "My New Year 2027 time capsule". The pen tool opens by default so kids can draw the portrait right on the screen, or you can print it blank and do everything by hand.

## Measure height the careful way

The height line is the one parents love most a few years later, so it is worth measuring it well. These steps follow the CDC's guide to <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/child-teen-calculator/measure-child-height-weight.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">measuring children's height at home</a>:

1. **Take off shoes, hats and bulky clothing**, and undo any hairstyle that sits on top of the head.
2. **Pick a hard floor and a plain wall.** Tile or wood works better than carpet, and the wall should have no baseboard molding where the child stands.
3. **Stand tall.** Feet flat and together, legs straight, arms at the sides, shoulders level, eyes looking straight ahead. Head, shoulders, bottom and heels touch the wall if your child's shape allows it.
4. **Use a book as a level.** Hold a hardcover book flat against the wall at a right angle and lower it until it rests firmly on the top of the head. Keep your own eyes level with the book.
5. **Mark and measure.** Lightly mark the wall under the book, then measure from the floor to the mark with a metal tape measure. Record it to the nearest 1/8 inch.

The Indian Health Service's <a href="https://www.ihs.gov/hwm/childguidelines" target="_blank" rel="noopener">child measuring guidelines</a> add two useful points for home: standing height is for children over 2 who can stand on their own, and a child under 6 is easier to measure with two adults, one holding the knees and feet gently in place. The same guide accepts two readings that agree within a quarter inch, so measure twice and write down either one.

## Turn it into a real time capsule

1. **Fill it in together.** Plan on 20 to 30 minutes. Younger kids can answer out loud while you write.
2. **Add a few small extras.** A hand tracing on the back, a ticket stub or a photo all fit in a letter envelope with the sheet.
3. **Seal and label it.** Write the "open again" date on the envelope too, and store it with other important papers so it does not get lost.
4. **Open it next New Year.** Fill in a fresh sheet first, then open last year's and compare answers side by side.

## Which parts fit which age

| Age | What your child does | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 4 and 5 | Draws the portrait, colors the pictures, says their favorites | Write the answers, take the height |
| 6 and 7 | Writes short favorites, copies the date | Help spell, take the height |
| 8 to 10 | Fills in everything, including both reflection boxes | Check the height with them and let them read the tape |
