The Sun sits in the top corner and all eight planets follow a dotted path in order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Each planet has a number and its name underneath, so a child who colors the sheet also practices the order. Type a name to turn the title into "Noah's Solar System", then tap to fill online or print it free.

## What kids get to color

- **The Sun:** a big round center with an inner circle and ten pointed rays.
- **Rocky planets:** Mercury, Venus, Earth with two land shapes and a little Moon, and Mars with two craters.
- **Giant planets:** Jupiter with four bands and a big oval spot, Saturn with two bands and a ring that goes behind and in front of it, Uranus with a ring standing almost upright, and Neptune with a center band.
- **The sky:** twelve stars to color in any shade.

That adds up to more than 50 separate shapes, so a preschooler can stop at the Sun and the big planets while an older child fills every band and star.

## A size order game built on real NASA numbers

The drawing is not to scale, which is a perfect opening for a game. Read the planet names from the table below in a random order and ask your child to point to where each one would rank by size. Then reveal the answer. Widths come from <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/planet-sizes-and-locations-in-our-solar-system/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">NASA's planet sizes and locations page</a>.

| Size rank | Planet | About how wide, compared with Earth | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jupiter | 11 times as wide | Gas giant |
| 2 | Saturn | 9 times as wide | Gas giant |
| 3 | Uranus | 4 times as wide | Ice giant |
| 4 | Neptune | 4 times as wide | Ice giant |
| 5 | Earth | 7,926 miles across | Rocky |
| 6 | Venus | About the same as Earth | Rocky |
| 7 | Mars | About half as wide | Rocky |
| 8 | Mercury | A little more than one third as wide | Rocky |

Ask a follow up: what do the four biggest planets have in common? They are the four outer planets. NASA groups Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars as rocky worlds with solid surfaces, Jupiter and Saturn as gas giants, and Uranus and Neptune as ice giants. A child can show this on the sheet by coloring a small dot or outline in one color on the rocky planets and another color on the giants.

## Space facts to read aloud while they color

Pick one fact per planet and read it as your child reaches that planet on the path. All of these come from NASA:

- Our solar system formed about 4.6 billion years ago.
- The Sun holds more than 99 percent of all the matter in the solar system.
- Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun.
- Venus is about as wide as Earth, which is why it is sometimes called Earth's twin. Mercury and Venus have no moons.
- Earth is about 93 million miles from the Sun on average.
- Mars, the red planet, has a canyon called Valles Marineris that is more than 2,000 miles long.
- Saturn is known for its icy rings.
- Neptune is the farthest planet, about 30 times as far from the Sun as Earth.

The <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/solar-system-facts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">NASA solar system facts page</a> has more for curious kids, including how far the solar system stretches beyond Neptune.
