Below the ice, two fish swim in the water, which happens to be what penguins hunt. Type a name and the title changes to "Leo's Penguin Family", then tap to fill online or print the sheet.

## Every part you can color

The big shapes are easy wins for little ones, and the small ones keep older kids busy.

- **Each grown-up penguin:** the back and head, the white front, two flippers, two feet, a beak and a patch on each side of the head.
- **The chick:** a round body, a face mask, a little front patch, two flippers, two feet and a beak.
- **The scene:** three icebergs, the ice shelf, two snow mounds, the water, two fish with tails, the sun and two clouds.

That is more than 40 separate shapes, so a 3 year old can color the penguins and the water while an 8 year old fills every iceberg and fish.

## How tall is an emperor penguin? A tape measure game

Emperors are the tallest of the 18 penguin species. SeaWorld's <a href="https://seaworld.org/animals/facts/birds/emperor-penguin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">emperor penguin fact sheet</a> lists them at up to 44 inches (112 cm) and 60 to 90 pounds. The smallest species, the little penguin, stands only 16 to 18 inches (41 to 45 cm) and weighs about 2.2 pounds.

Turn those numbers into a game that takes five minutes:

1. Stick a strip of painter's tape on a wall or door frame at 44 inches from the floor. Write "Emperor" on it.
2. Add a second strip at 17 inches and write "Little penguin".
3. Have your child stand against the wall. Are they taller than an emperor yet? Mark their height with a third strip.
4. Ask: "How many little penguins would you need to stack to reach the emperor?" (A bit more than two and a half.)
5. Draw your child next to the penguin family on the back of the sheet, at the right size.

| Penguin | Height | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Emperor (tallest) | Up to 44 in (112 cm) | 60 to 90 lb |
| Little penguin (smallest) | 16 to 18 in (41 to 45 cm) | About 2.2 lb |

## Five true penguin facts to read while coloring

These come from the <a href="https://www.antarctica.gov.au/about-antarctica/animals/penguins/emperor-penguin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Australian Antarctic Program's emperor penguin page</a> and SeaWorld's guides.

- **Winter babies.** Emperor penguins are the only animals that breed during the Antarctic winter.
- **Dads hold the egg.** Only the males keep the egg warm. A father balances a single egg on top of his feet, under a warm fold of belly skin called a brood patch. He eats nothing during this time and can lose up to 45% of his body weight.
- **Penguin huddles.** To stay warm, the males crowd together and take turns moving between the cold edge and the warm middle. Huddling can cut the heat they lose by as much as half.
- **Fish for dinner.** Their most common food is a small fish called Antarctic silverfish. They also eat krill and some squid, which is why the fish on this sheet are a good place for a story.
- **Deep divers.** Most dives last 3 to 6 minutes. The longest one on record lasted 22 minutes, and the deepest reached 565 meters, roughly 1,850 feet.

## Why penguins wear black and white

Ask your child why a penguin might have a dark back and a white front. SeaWorld explains that all adult penguins are countershaded. From above, the dark back blends in with the deep ocean. From below, the white front blends in with the bright surface of the water. Try coloring one penguin in true black and white, then make the other one any colors your child likes and talk about which one would be easier to spot in the sea.

Look at photos of real emperors together before coloring the patches on the sides of the head and the beak, so your child can choose colors that match the real birds.
