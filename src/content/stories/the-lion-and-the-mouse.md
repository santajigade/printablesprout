On a warm, sunny day, a big lion lay down under a tree. He closed his eyes. Soon he was fast asleep. Zzz.

A tiny grey mouse came scampering by. She did not see that the lump in the grass was a lion! Up she ran, over his tail. Down she ran, across his back. Up and down, up and down.

## The Lion Wakes Up

The lion opened one eye. Then he opened the other eye. "Who is tickling me?" he roared.

Plop! He put his big paw down, and the little mouse was stuck under it.

"Oh, please let me go, King Lion!" squeaked the mouse. "I did not mean to wake you. If you let me go, I promise I will help you one day."

The lion laughed and laughed. "You? Help me? You are so small, and I am so big!"

But the idea made him chuckle, and he felt kind. So he lifted his paw. "Off you go, little one," he said.

"Thank you!" said the mouse, and away she ran.

## Caught in the Hunters' Ropes

Some days later, hunters came to the grassland. They wanted to take the lion to the King's town. They set a trap, and the lion walked right into it.

The hunters tied him up with strong ropes and tied him to a tree. Then they went off to fetch a wagon.

The lion pulled. He wiggled. He tugged. But the ropes held tight. "Oh dear," he sighed. "Who can help me now?"

## A Small Friend With Strong Teeth

Pitter, patter. Along came the little mouse. She saw the lion tied to the tree.

"Do not worry, King Lion," she said. "I made you a promise."

She began to gnaw the ropes with her sharp little teeth. Nibble, nibble, nibble. Snap went one rope. Snap went another. Soon every rope fell away, and the lion was free!

<figure><img src="/stories/the-lion-and-the-mouse/brave-mouse-chewing-ropes-to-free-lion.svg" alt="The little mouse chewing through the hunters ropes to set the trapped lion free beside a tree" width="1200" height="800" loading="lazy" /></figure>

"You laughed at me," said the mouse with a smile. "But was I not right?"

"You were right," said the lion. "Thank you, my friend."

From that day on, the big lion and the little mouse were the best of friends. Little friends can be great friends.

## Be a Helpful Mouse: The Knot Nibble Game

The mouse freed the lion one rope at a time. Try this hands-on game together (about 10 minutes):

1. A grown up ties 3 or 4 loose, easy knots along a piece of yarn or a soft scarf. This is the "lion's rope."
2. Your child is the little mouse. Can they untie each knot with their fingers? Say "Nibble, nibble, snap!" each time a knot comes loose.
3. Count the knots together as they go: one, two, three, four.
4. When the rope is free, take turns: the child ties a knot for the grown up to untie.
5. Talk about it: what is one small way you can help someone big at home today?

If your child liked this fable, try another one: [The Tortoise and the Hare](/stories/the-tortoise-and-the-hare/).
