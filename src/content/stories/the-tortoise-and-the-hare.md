Long ago, in a green meadow, there lived a Hare with very long legs and a Tortoise with a very round shell.

The Hare loved to run. Zip! He could cross the whole meadow before you could say "carrot."

The Tortoise did everything slowly. He walked slowly. He ate slowly. He even blinked slowly.

## A Boastful Hare and a Brave Idea

One sunny morning the Hare hopped up to the Tortoise and laughed.

"Do you ever get anywhere, Tortoise?" he said. "By the time you reach the pond, the pond will be gone!"

The other animals giggled. The Tortoise did not get upset. He just smiled.

"I get where I am going," he said. "Let's have a race and see."

The Hare laughed so hard that his ears flopped. "A race? With you? All right, just for fun."

The wise old Fox said she would be the judge. She marked a path from the big rock, past the oak tree, all the way to the red flag on the hill.

## Ready, Set, Go!

"Ready," said the Fox. "Set. GO!"

Whoosh! The Hare shot off like the wind. In a blink he was far, far away. All anyone could see was a little cloud of dust.

The Tortoise lifted one foot. Then another foot. Then another. Step, step, step.

The Hare looked back. The Tortoise was so far behind that he looked like a tiny green pebble.

"This is too easy," said the Hare. "I will rest under this oak tree. When that slowpoke comes near, I will zoom past him and win."

He lay down in the cool shade. The grass was soft. The bees buzzed a sleepy song. Soon the Hare was snoring.

## Step by Step Past the Oak Tree

The sun climbed high in the sky. The Tortoise was hot and his legs were tired.

"Keep going," he told himself. "Step, step, step."

He did not stop to rest. He did not stop to nibble flowers. He did not stop to chat. He just kept going.

Step by step, he reached the oak tree. There was the Hare, fast asleep. The Tortoise walked quietly past him, step, step, step, and up the hill.

## Who Wins the Race?

At last the Hare woke up and stretched. He looked around. Where was the Tortoise?

Then he saw him. The Tortoise was almost at the red flag!

The Hare jumped up and ran faster than he had ever run before. His feet flew. His ears flapped. But it was too late.

The Tortoise took one last step and crossed the line. The Fox waved the flag. "The Tortoise wins!"

All the animals cheered.

<figure><img src="/stories/the-tortoise-and-the-hare/tortoise-wins-race-at-red-flag-hare-running-behind.svg" alt="The tortoise crossing the finish line by the red flag as the hare races up behind him too late" width="1200" height="800" loading="lazy" /></figure>

The Hare sat down, puffing and panting. He felt a little silly. Then he held out his paw.

"You won, fair and square," he said. "I was fast, but you never gave up."

The Tortoise shook his paw and smiled. "Maybe next time," he said, "we can walk to the pond together."

And they did, slowly and happily, side by side.

## Play the Slow and Steady Challenge

The Tortoise won because he kept going a little at a time. Try the same idea at home this week:

1. Pick one thing your child is learning, like tying shoes, a tricky puzzle or reading a short book.
2. Practice for just 5 minutes each day. Set a kitchen timer so it feels like a game.
3. After each try, add a sticker to a [reward sticker chart](/reward-sticker-chart/) or draw one tortoise step on paper.
4. At the end of the week, look back at all the steps together and talk about how far your child has come.
5. For a silly bonus, hold a family "slowest walk" race across the room. The last one to the finish line wins!
