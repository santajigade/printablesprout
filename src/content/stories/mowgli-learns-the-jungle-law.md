Mowgli lived with the wolves in the Seeonee hills of India. He was growing fast, and every day he had lessons with his teacher, Baloo, the big brown bear.

Baloo taught him the Law of the Jungle. Never trust a rotten branch. Speak politely to the wild bees. Warn the water snakes before you splash into their pool. Mowgli had to say every rule again and again until he knew it by heart.

## The Master Words

The most important lesson was the Master Words. "If you say these words the right way," said Baloo, "the animals of the jungle will know you are a friend."

For the Hunting People, Mowgli said them in Baloo's deep bear voice: "We be of one blood, ye and I." For the Birds, he said the same words and ended with a kite's whistle. For the Snake People, he gave the special Snake's Call.

Bagheera, the black panther, lay on a branch and watched. "You are too strict with him, Baloo," he purred. "He is only a little man-cub."

"Better that he learns from me now," said Baloo, "than gets hurt later because he did not know."

Mowgli sat up proudly. "One day I will have my own tribe," he said. "The Bandar-log told me so. They gave me nuts and said I could be their leader!"

Baloo and Bagheera looked at each other. The Bandar-log were the monkeys.

"Listen to me, Man-cub," said Baloo seriously. "The monkeys have no Law. They have no leader. They make big plans, then forget them a minute later. The rest of the jungle does not play with them."

## Carried Away Through the Treetops

At midday, Mowgli fell asleep between Baloo and Bagheera. When he woke up, he was flying through the treetops!

Lots of monkeys had grabbed his arms and legs. They swung him from branch to branch, laughing and shouting. Mowgli was dizzy and a little scared.

High above, he saw a bird circling. It was Rann the kite. Mowgli remembered his lesson and called out the Master Words for the Birds, ending with the kite's whistle.

"Mark my trail!" he shouted. "Tell Baloo and Bagheera where I am!"

Rann whistled back, and he followed the monkeys from high in the sky.

## Kaa Joins the Search

Back on the ground, Baloo and Bagheera were very worried. "We cannot catch the monkeys in the trees," said Bagheera. "But I know who they fear."

They went to find Kaa, the great rock python. He was about thirty feet long, and his new skin shone in the sun. The monkeys were more afraid of Kaa than of anyone else, because Kaa could creep up on them in the dark.

"Kaa," said Baloo politely, "the Bandar-log have taken our man-cub. Will you help us?"

Kaa had heard that the monkeys called him rude names, so he agreed. Just then Rann swooped down. "The monkeys have taken him to the Cold Lairs!" he called.

The Cold Lairs was an old, empty city hidden in the jungle. Its walls were falling down, and trees grew through its palace floors.

## Rescue at the Cold Lairs

At the Cold Lairs, the monkeys showed off. They danced and sang and boasted. They brought Mowgli fruit, then fought over it and forgot to give it to him. Mowgli felt hungry and tired. "Baloo was right," he thought. "They have no Law at all."

That night, under the moon, Bagheera crept in. Crowds of monkeys rushed at him, chattering and tugging, and pushed Mowgli inside a small white marble summer house. It was full of cobras! But Mowgli gave the Snake's Call. "Down hoods all," said the cobras, and they let him be.

"Roll to the water tanks, Bagheera!" called Mowgli. Bagheera splashed into the water, where the monkeys would not follow him. Then Baloo arrived to help.

At last, a long dark shape slid over the wall. "It is Kaa!" screamed the monkeys. "Run!" They fled in every direction.

Kaa hit the marble wall of the summer house with his strong nose, again and again, until it broke open. Mowgli jumped out and hugged Baloo and Bagheera tight.

<figure><img src="/stories/mowgli-learns-the-jungle-law/kaa-the-python-helps-baloo-and-bagheera-rescue-mowgli.svg" alt="Kaa the big rock python by moonlight in the old ruined city as Baloo the bear, Mowgli and Bagheera the panther stand together and the monkeys run away" width="1200" height="800" loading="lazy" /></figure>

In the moonlight, Kaa began to sway in slow loops and curls. The monkeys stood frozen, staring, unable to move. Even Baloo and Bagheera began to feel sleepy. So Mowgli took his friends by the fur and led them safely away into the jungle.

Before they left, Mowgli thanked Kaa. "You have a brave heart and a courteous tongue," said the python.

On the way home, Mowgli said sorry. "I should have listened," he said. "The monkeys only said nice things to trick me." Baloo and Bagheera forgave him, and by the Law of the Jungle, once a thing is settled, nobody nags about it afterward. Mowgli fell asleep on Bagheera's warm back and woke up safe in his own cave.

## Make Your Family's Master Words and Jungle Laws

Baloo's lessons saved Mowgli. Make your own set of family "laws" and a secret phrase (about 20 minutes):

1. Choose your family's Master Words, a short silly phrase only your family knows, like Mowgli's "We be of one blood, ye and I." Practice saying it in a bear voice, a bird whistle and a snake hiss.
2. Together, write five Family Jungle Laws on a big sheet of paper, such as "We use kind words" and "We tell a grown up where we are going."
3. Draw a jungle border around your laws: a bear, a panther, a kite and a long python.
4. Quiz each other at dinner, just like Baloo. Can everyone say all five laws by heart?
5. Talk about it: why did Mowgli's teacher make him repeat the rules so many times?
