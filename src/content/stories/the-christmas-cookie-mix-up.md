Ten days before Christmas, the whole kitchen smelled like butter and sugar.

Eli stood on a stool next to Dad. Trays of warm star cookies covered the table. Bowls of white, red and green icing waited in a row.

"These are for the neighbors," said Dad. "One box for every house on our street."

Eli picked up a cookie and sniffed it. Mmm. <span data-p="subj">He</span> already had a plan.

## The Fanciest Cookies Ever

Eli worked very hard. <span data-p="subj">He</span> gave one star a red coat of icing and gold sprinkles. <span data-p="subj">He</span> made one look like a snowflake, with tiny white dots. <span data-p="subj">He</span> made one with a smiling face and green candy eyes.

They were the most beautiful cookies <span data-p="subj">he</span> had ever made.

"These ones are mine," Eli whispered. <span data-p="subj">He</span> put the fancy cookies in a shiny blue box. On the lid <span data-p="subj">he</span> wrote a label in big letters: ELI. KEEP OUT!

The plain cookies went into red boxes for the neighbors.

<span data-p="poss">His</span> little sister, Rosie, wanted to help. She stacked the boxes. She tied the ribbons. She even put a bow on the blue box, because blue was her favorite color.

## Rosie's Big Mix-Up

That afternoon, Dad and Eli carried the boxes down the street. Knock, knock. "Merry Christmas!"

The last house belonged to Mr. Ortiz. He lived alone, and his family lived far away. Rosie handed him the box with the prettiest bow.

It was the blue one.

Eli's mouth fell open. <span data-p="poss">His</span> fancy cookies! <span data-p="subj">He</span> wanted to shout, "Wait, those are mine!"

But then Mr. Ortiz lifted the lid.

<figure><img src="/stories/the-christmas-cookie-mix-up/neighbor-opening-blue-cookie-box-at-snowy-front-door.svg" alt="Mr. Ortiz smiling as he opens a blue box of fancy cookies at his snowy front door while Eli and Rosie watch" width="1200" height="800" loading="lazy" /></figure>

## The Best Gift on the Street

Mr. Ortiz looked at the red star with gold sprinkles. He looked at the snowflake and the smiling face. His eyes grew shiny.

"My grandchildren used to make cookies like these with me," he said softly. "I thought nobody would remember me this year."

Eli felt something warm in <span data-p="poss">his</span> chest. It felt even better than eating a cookie.

"I made them," <span data-p="subj">he</span> said. "Merry Christmas, Mr. Ortiz."

Walking home, Rosie looked worried. "Did I give away your cookies?"

Eli laughed and took her hand. "You gave them to exactly the right person."

## A New Christmas Tradition

The next Saturday, there was a knock at the door. It was Mr. Ortiz, holding a bag of flour and a big old cookie cutter shaped like a bell.

"I heard there is a great cookie maker in this house," he said with a wink.

Eli grinned. <span data-p="subj">He</span> pulled out a stool for Mr. Ortiz, right next to <span data-p="poss">his</span> own.

That afternoon they made bells, stars and snowflakes. This time Eli made the fanciest cookies of all and put every single one in a box to give away.

## Make a Cookie Kindness Box

Turn the story into a giving project this December. It takes about 30 minutes, plus delivery:

1. Talk together about someone who might like a surprise: a neighbor, a grandparent, a teacher or a crossing guard.
2. Bake simple cookies with a grown up, or use store bought ones and decorate them with icing and sprinkles.
3. Put them in a small box or paper bag and let your child design a label with the person's name.
4. Tuck in a handwritten note. Our free [Christmas thank you notes](/christmas-thank-you-notes-for-kids/) have fill in the blank lines that even early writers can finish.
5. Deliver the box together and talk afterward about how it felt to give.

No baking time? Color a [gingerbread house coloring page](/gingerbread-house-coloring-page/) and give it as a card instead.
