The Christmas lantern parade was tonight, and Nora could not wait.

Every year, Grandpa's three reindeer walked down Main Street pulling a little red sleigh. This year, Nora was going to walk in front with a glowing lantern.

"Time to go!" said Grandpa, buckling the red harnesses with the jingly bells. Maple was ready. Birch was ready. But where was Juniper, the youngest reindeer?

## Hurry Up, Juniper!

Juniper was at the far end of the yard, with her nose in the snow.

"Come on, Juniper!" called Nora. <span data-p="subj">She</span> stamped <span data-p="poss">her</span> boots. "We will be late!"

Juniper did not look up. Scrape, scrape went her hooves.

Nora grabbed the rope and pulled. "Hurry up!"

But Juniper planted her four hooves in the snow and would not move one step. The harder Nora pulled, the stiller Juniper stood.

"Pulling never works with a reindeer," said Grandpa gently. "Let's wait and watch."

## What Juniper Was Looking For

So Nora let go of the rope. <span data-p="subj">She</span> took a deep breath, the way Grandpa had shown <span data-p="obj">her</span>. In, two, three. Out, two, three. Then <span data-p="subj">she</span> knelt down very quietly and looked.

Under the snow, Juniper had found a soft, pale green clump.

"That is lichen," whispered Grandpa. "Some people call it reindeer moss. In winter, reindeer scrape away the snow with their hooves to find it."

"She was hungry," said Nora softly. "She wasn't being naughty at all."

<figure><img src="/stories/the-reindeer-who-was-late/girl-waiting-patiently-while-little-reindeer-eats-lichen.svg" alt="Nora standing quietly in the snow while little Juniper the reindeer scrapes the snow and eats lichen" width="1200" height="800" loading="lazy" /></figure>

Nora waited. <span data-p="subj">She</span> counted snowflakes on <span data-p="poss">her</span> mitten. <span data-p="subj">She</span> hummed a Christmas song. At last, Juniper lifted her head, gave a happy snort and trotted over all by herself.

## A Spot in the Middle

At the edge of town, Main Street was full of lights and noise. Juniper's ears twitched. She took a step back.

"She is scared," said Nora. "What can we do?"

Grandpa smiled. "Reindeer like to stay close together in a herd. Let's put her in the middle, with her big sisters."

So Maple walked on one side and Birch walked on the other. Little Juniper walked right between them, safe and snug.

"And I will light the way," said Nora, lifting <span data-p="poss">her</span> lantern high.

<figure><img src="/stories/the-reindeer-who-was-late/reindeer-team-and-girl-with-lantern-in-christmas-parade.svg" alt="Three reindeer pulling a little red sleigh with a Christmas tree down a snowy Main Street while Nora leads with a glowing lantern" width="1200" height="800" loading="lazy" /></figure>

Jingle, jingle went the bells. The crowd cheered as the little red sleigh glided by. Nobody even noticed that the youngest reindeer had almost been late.

That night, Nora gave Juniper an extra pat. "Thank you for teaching me to wait," <span data-p="subj">she</span> said.

Juniper nuzzled <span data-p="poss">her</span> mitten. Some things are worth waiting for.

## Play the Reindeer Herd Walk

Juniper felt brave when she walked in the middle of her herd. Try this game with two or more kids (about 10 minutes):

1. Line up in a row like Maple, Juniper and Birch. Hold hands or hold a soft scarf as the "harness."
2. A grown up calls "Walk," "Trot" or "Stop." The herd must move together, so nobody gets left behind.
3. Take turns being Juniper in the middle and Nora at the front with a flashlight "lantern."
4. Patience round: the grown up says "Find the lichen!" and everyone freezes and counts to 10 slowly before the herd may walk again.
5. Talk about real reindeer: both boy and girl reindeer grow antlers, and the girls keep theirs all through the winter. That is why Maple, Birch and Juniper still have antlers at Christmas!
