It was seven o'clock on a warm evening in the Seeonee hills of India. Father Wolf woke up from his long day's sleep, stretched his paws and yawned.

Beside him lay Mother Wolf, with her four little cubs tumbling and squeaking around her. The moon shone into the mouth of their cave.

"It is time to go hunting," said Father Wolf.

## A Visitor With Bad News

Just then a thin shadow slipped into the cave. It was Tabaqui the jackal, who loved gossip and making trouble. The wolves did not like him much, but they were polite.

Tabaqui chewed an old bone and grinned. "Have you heard? Shere Khan the tiger is coming to hunt in these hills!"

Father Wolf growled. Shere Khan usually lived far away, by the Waingunga River. By the Law of the Jungle, he should not hunt here without warning.

"Shere Khan has been lame in one foot since the day he was born," said Mother Wolf. "That is why they call him Lungri, the Lame One. He is grumpy, and he makes trouble for everyone."

Soon they heard an angry roar from the valley below. Shere Khan had come too close to a woodcutters' campfire and burned his paws. Now he was cross, and the woodcutters had run away into the night.

## A Surprise at the Cave

Father Wolf crouched at the cave door, ready to jump at whatever was coming up the hill. Then he stopped, right in the middle of his leap.

Standing in the moonlight, holding on to a low branch, was a tiny brown baby. He was just big enough to walk. He looked up at Father Wolf, and he laughed.

<figure><img src="/stories/mowgli-joins-the-wolf-pack/father-wolf-finds-baby-mowgli-by-moonlight.svg" alt="A grey wolf in the moonlit jungle finding a laughing little boy holding on to a low branch" width="1200" height="800" loading="lazy" /></figure>

"A man's cub!" said Father Wolf. "Look!"

Very, very gently, the way wolves carry their own pups, Father Wolf picked up the baby and set him down among the cubs. The little boy wiggled right in to snuggle against Mother Wolf's warm fur.

"How little he is," said Mother Wolf softly. "And how bold!"

## Mother Wolf Says No to the Tiger

Suddenly the moonlight went dark. Shere Khan's huge striped head was pushed into the cave door, and Tabaqui squeaked behind him.

"A man's cub came this way," snarled the tiger. "He is mine. Give him to me!"

Father Wolf stood tall. "We wolves are a Free People. We take orders from the Head of our Pack, not from a tiger."

Then Mother Wolf rose up, her eyes shining green in the dark. Her name was Raksha, which means The Demon, and right now she looked just like it.

"This cub is mine," she said. "He will live with my family and run with the Pack. I will call him Mowgli the Frog. Now go away, Lame One!"

Shere Khan knew that a mother wolf protecting her cubs is a very fierce thing. He backed out of the cave, grumbling. "We will see what the Pack says!"

## Look Well, O Wolves!

The Law of the Jungle said that every new cub must be shown to the whole Pack at the Council Rock, so every wolf would know him. The meeting was held when the moon was full.

On that night, the wolf family carried Mowgli up to a hilltop covered with big rocks. Akela, the great grey Lone Wolf who led the Pack, lay stretched out on his rock. Forty wolves sat in a circle below.

One by one, the cubs stepped into the middle. "Look well," called Akela. "Look well, O Wolves!"

Mowgli sat in the moonlight, happily playing with some shiny pebbles.

From behind the rocks came Shere Khan's roar. "That cub is mine!"

The Law said that if anyone argued about a cub, two members of the Pack, who were not his parents, had to speak up for him. "Who speaks for this cub?" asked Akela. For a moment, nobody answered.

## A Bear and a Panther Speak Up

Then a sleepy brown bear stood up on his back legs. It was Baloo, the wise old teacher who taught the wolf cubs the Law of the Jungle.

"I speak for the man's cub," rumbled Baloo. "He will do no harm. Let him run with the Pack, and I will teach him myself."

"We need one more," said Akela.

A black shadow dropped silently into the circle. It was Bagheera the panther, sleek as midnight, with a voice as soft as honey. Everyone respected Bagheera.

"The Law says a cub may be welcomed if someone pays a price," purred Bagheera. "I will add a fat bull to Baloo's good word. It is lying just half a mile from here, ready for a feast."

The wolves liked the sound of that. "Let him stay!" they cried. And off they ran to the feast.

Shere Khan roared in anger, but no one listened to him now.

So Mowgli became a member of the Seeonee Wolf Pack, thanks to a mother who loved him, a bear who believed in him and a panther who spoke up for him. He grew up strong and clever with his four wolf brothers, and Baloo taught him every rule of the jungle.

## Hold a Family Council Rock Night

The wolves welcomed Mowgli at a special meeting. Try a cozy version at home after dinner (about 15 minutes):

1. Sit in a circle on the floor, like the wolves at the Council Rock. Dim the lights and use a flashlight as the "moon."
2. Take turns being Akela, the leader who calls out, "Look well!"
3. When it is your turn in the middle, everyone else says one thing they love about you, just like Baloo and Bagheera spoke up for Mowgli.
4. Together, make three "Pack Promises" for your family, such as "We help each other" or "We speak kindly." Write them on paper and hang them up.
5. Older kids can read more of Kipling's The Jungle Book with a grown up and pick their favorite jungle animal.

If your kids liked this story, try another classic: [The Tortoise and the Hare](/stories/the-tortoise-and-the-hare/).
